## Notes on individual decisions

**ADR-0002 rationale.** The blueprint's "Employment #1 / #2" is satisfied today by lifecycle spells on one Employee; splitting employees per spell would duplicate identity and break the lifetime record. A future `employments` table is additive (`employee_positions.employment_id`) if separate contracts must carry separate codes or legal entities.

**ADR-0004 rationale for manager visibility.** A manager who cannot see a direct report cannot approve, review or coach; reporting relationships are audited, effective-dated and set by HR, so they are a safe basis for access. Indirect reports are intentionally not included by default.

**ADR-0005 rationale.** A generic field matrix would duplicate the permission catalogue; classification in config plus per-model masking is testable and sufficient until a tenant needs per-field configuration.

**ADR-0013 rationale.** Interface + middleware is the smallest mechanically enforceable pattern: the architecture test checks every `ShouldQueue` class in `app/`, so a new job cannot be merged without carrying its tenant.

**ADR-0014 API-key scope.** Data contract: `api_keys.access_scope` JSON nullable with the same shape as `user_access_scopes` grouped by dimension; enforcement: `AuthenticateApiKey` registers a synthetic principal in `AccessScopes` so the same global scope applies. Not built now because no consumer needs it before the Integration Hub.

## SaaS.5 decisions

Recorded by SaaS.5 (Commercial Packaging, Limits & Pricing Architecture, 6 October 2026). Status **Accepted (shadow)**: in force for the catalogue and the shadow engine; nothing is enforced. Report: `docs/saas/SaaS-5-Commercial-Packaging-Report.md`.

**No business policy was decided.** Packaging content, limit values, prices, trials, plan migrations, a default plan, limit modes (D-16) and operator roles stay open. The decisions below are structural.

| ADR | Title | Status | Decision (one line) | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0035 | The limit model: one catalogue, explicit states | Accepted (shadow) | A limit is a `Capability` (no second catalogue). The enum gives its key, unit, module, whether its usage is measured today (only `active_employees.max`) and its minimum (1 for the protected employee limit, else 0). A plan value is a whole number of the unit, or NULL for unlimited; an absent limit is "not set", with no agreed limit (UNKNOWN, `LIMIT_NOT_CONFIGURED`). A limit inside a commercial module (AI requests, API requests) never outlives its module: while the module is denied, the limit is not included (DENY, `MODULE_NOT_ENTITLED`). The seven limit states (unlimited, not included, not set, no commercial answer, unmeasured, within, exceeded) map to distinct outcome and reason pairs. Limit modes (hard or soft) and the meaning of "per month" are left to metering (WS4) | `CommercialPackagingTest`, architecture test (measured = observed) |
| 0036 | A published plan version is a consistent package | Accepted (shadow) | Publication refuses a version that includes a feature without its module, or sets a limit of a commercial module without that module. A draft may pass through such states; Platform › Plans shows why it cannot be published yet. The rule follows from the catalogue's structure; it is not a packaging policy | `CommercialPackagingTest` |
| 0037 | Price is not part of a plan or of entitlement | Accepted (shadow) | A plan says what a tenant gets, never what it costs. A future price will be its own versioned definition referencing a published plan version: chosen by a subscription, read by billing, never read by the entitlement engine, and never a condition of an entitlement decision. **No price table is created** until the price decisions exist: D-1 pricing metric, D-3 currencies and markets, the billing intervals offered, D-10 tax-inclusive display, D-12 free plan (with WS3 and WS5 as consumers) | Architecture test: no money columns in the plan tables, no pricing or billing in entitlement code |
